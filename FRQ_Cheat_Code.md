# AP Chemistry FRQ "Cheat Code" Guide
**Strategic Pattern Recognition & Point-Harvesting Phrases**

---

## 🔴 1. Acids & Bases (Unit 8) - *Your #1 Weakness*
**Trigger:** You see a Titration Curve (S-shape graph).

*   **The Half-Equivalence Trick:** 
    *   **Question:** "What is the $pK_a$ of the acid?"
    *   **Cheat Code:** Find the **Equivalence Point** (the vertical part of the curve). Go to exactly **HALF** of that volume on the x-axis. The pH at that exact point **IS** the $pK_a$.
    *   **Must-Say Phrase:** "At the half-equivalence point, $[HA] = [A^-]$, therefore $pH = pK_a$."

*   **The Particle Diagram:**
    *   **Question:** "Draw/Identify the species in solution before/after equivalence."
    *   **Cheat Code:** Before half-equivalence, there is more $[HA]$. After half-equivalence, there is more $[A^-]$.
    *   **Must-Say Phrase:** "Before the half-equivalence point, more acid particles are present than conjugate base particles."

## 🟠 2. Thermodynamics II & Favorability (Unit 9)
**Trigger:** Question asks about "Thermodynamic Favorability" or $\Delta G$.

*   **The Entropy ($\Delta S$) Explanation:**
    *   **Question:** "Why is the entropy change positive?"
    *   **Must-Say Phrase:** "The products are **more dispersed** and **occupy a greater number of microstates** than the reactants (especially if a gas is formed from a solid/liquid)."

*   **The $\Delta G$ Temperature Dependence:**
    *   **Question:** "Is this favorable at all temperatures?"
    *   **Cheat Code:** Use the equation $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$.
    *   **Must-Say Phrase:** "The reaction is only favorable at high/low temperatures such that the magnitude of $T\Delta S$ is greater than $\Delta H$."

## 🟡 3. Intermolecular Forces (Unit 3)
**Trigger:** Comparing Boiling Points, Vapor Pressure, or Solubility.

*   **The "Polarizability" Trap:**
    *   **Question:** "Why does $C_8H_{18}$ have a higher boiling point than $CH_4$?"
    *   **Must-Say Phrase:** "$C_8H_{18}$ has a **larger electron cloud** which is **more polarizable**, leading to **stronger London Dispersion Forces**." (NEVER just say "it's bigger").

*   **The "Coulombic" Power-Word:**
    *   **Question:** "Explain the difference in [Atomic Property]."
    *   **Must-Say Phrase:** "The electrons experience **weaker/stronger Coulombic attractions** because they are [farther from/closer to] the nucleus and have [more/fewer] occupied shells."

## 🟢 4. Electrochemistry (Unit 4 & 9)
**Trigger:** You see a Battery (Galvanic Cell) or Electrolysis.

*   **The Favorability Link:**
    *   **Question:** "Is the reaction thermodynamically favorable?"
    *   **Must-Say Phrase:** "$E^\circ_{cell}$ is positive, which means $\Delta G^\circ$ is negative (via $\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ$), therefore the reaction is thermodynamically favorable."

*   **The "Electrolysis" Calculation:**
    *   **Cheat Code:** Use the units as a map. $Amps = Coulombs/Second$. Use $96,485$ (Faraday's Constant) to convert between Coulombs and Moles of Electrons.

## 🔵 5. Kinetics (Unit 5)
**Trigger:** You see a data table of Concentration vs. Time.

*   **The "Line" Method:**
    *   **Cheat Code:** 
        *   If $[A]$ vs $t$ is linear $\rightarrow$ **0th Order**.
        *   If $\ln[A]$ vs $t$ is linear $\rightarrow$ **1st Order**.
        *   If $1/[A]$ vs $t$ is linear $\rightarrow$ **2nd Order**.
    *   **Must-Say Phrase:** "The plot of [insert linear one] versus time is the most linear, indicating the reaction is [order] with respect to [reactant]."
